The tune — four goes

You said let’s try again. The tune itself was already built the way that plumber’s one is — rests on the downbeats, a middle that drops away — so what I changed is the instrument carrying it, which is what was making it sound like a music box. Two and three go further.

1 — what is in the game now
The melody back on a square wave with a soft one under it. This is the change; the three below are alternatives.
2 — what it was before
The soft one you heard and asked me to try again on, so you can hear what moved rather than take my word for it.
3 — bounce
A shuffle: the off-beats land late, which is most of what “bouncy” means. Shorter, catchier melody over a walking bass.
4 — warm
No edge anywhere, a gentle swing, and no drums at all until half way through. Closest to the covers of that plumber’s tune played on real instruments.

The crackling

Three likelier suspects measured and cleared: the music never gets within a factor of three of clipping, the aliasing from the pitch shift is half a per cent of the signal, and the bitrate is fine. What was left is that the synthesiser normalises every clip to the very top of the scale, so thirty-one of the eighty-four files went out with samples at or past full scale — and a phone playing that under a game has nowhere to put them.
